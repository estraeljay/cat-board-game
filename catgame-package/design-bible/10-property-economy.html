10. Property Economy

All questions resolved

§ What's locked

The inflation concern was specifically flagged, so this needs to be finalized carefully.

  • Working principle: real-money property purchase ≠ direct creation of tradable in-game coins.
  • ✓ Two currencies exist: Fel — persistent, cross-game, used for property leveling — and Coins — tracked per-match, but whatever a player finishes a match holding becomes theirs permanently (never reset to zero, never pooled with teammates, even in a shared win).
  • ✓ Gems are the premium, real-money-only currency and can be spent to buy Fel directly (see Section 16).
  • ✓ Fel sources: winning a Prized Game (claims 80% of the entry-fee pot, which now scales with the match's chosen stake tier — see Section 22), a small starting grant for new players, small Campaign-mode rewards, and Gem purchases.
  • ✓ Initial property purchase is paid in Gems (Section 09/16) — Gems are primarily real-money, with a small in-game earn path via bonuses/events.
  • ✓ Rent: visitors pay the property owner directly in Coins on visit (proposed) — a new, direct Coin sink/source pair beyond property card draws.
  • ✓ Fel sink: property leveling costs (Section 11's table) — the main place Fel actually leaves circulation.
  • ✓ Second Fel sink: the entry-fee pot now burns 20% at match end (Section 22) — this is a deliberate, ongoing supply reduction on top of property leveling, not just zero-sum recirculation.
  • ✓ Coin source: property card draws — cards can award Coins depending on what's drawn (amount TBD, connects to Section 12).
  • ✓ Coin transfer on elimination: 80/20 split to the PvP winner (locked in Section 15).
Primary exploit risk — largely addressed Player-to-player property trading for currency was flagged as the game's primary economic exploit risk. The Fel entry-fee pot being zero-sum among players (it recirculates rather than being generated from nothing) substantially reduces this risk for the leveling economy specifically. The property resale market (buyer/seller mechanics, fees) is still fully open below and deserves the same scrutiny once designed.

§ Resolved decisions

  • How properties generate value for their owner: Direct Coin rent from visitors, plus indirect value via visit-driven leveling (Section 09/11).
  • Whether resale sales create coins: No — a zero-sum Gem transfer, nothing new created (protects against inflation, same principle as the entry-fee pot).
  • Whether the buyer pays coins/Fel or real money: Neither — buyer pays in Gems, matching the original purchase currency.
  • Whether the seller receives coins/Fel: No — seller receives Gems directly from the buyer.
  • Market transaction fees: 5% resale fee charged on property sales.
  • Coin sinks: Property leveling Fel cost (Section 11) is the primary sink.
  • Coin sources: Entry-fee pot wins, starting grants, Campaign rewards, property card draws, Gem purchases.
  • Maximum coin/Fel supply behavior: Now has two real sinks (property leveling costs + the 20% pot burn) working against Gem-purchase/Campaign inflow — a hard cap probably isn't needed on top of that, but should be revisited once real numbers (rent rates, leveling thresholds) exist to model actual supply/demand.
  • Coins have exactly three uses, nothing else: paying rent when stepping on a property, serving as the leveling-threshold measure for property leveling (Section 11), and paying to use the Inn, Blacksmith, and Church (Section 02/04/07) — the Fountain stays free, since it's a reward-granting location, not a paid service. Coins remain purely functional, not a general spending currency.
  • Whether coins are persistent or match-specific: Both, in a sense — tracked per-match, but the ending total becomes the player's permanently. Fel is fully persistent/cross-game.
  • Whether coins survive elimination: Yes, minus the 80% taken by whoever eliminated you (Section 15).
  • Whether resale prices fluctuate: Yes, inherently — since the market is player-driven, sellers set their own asking price rather than following a fixed algorithmic formula.
  • Player-driven, not NPC-controlled: Owners list properties directly, other players buy them — a real player-to-player market, not an algorithmic buyback.
