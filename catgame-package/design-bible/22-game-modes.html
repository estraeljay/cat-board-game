22. Game Modes

§ What's locked

Everything defined in Sections 01–21 so far describes one specific way to play — the paid, high-stakes version. This section splits that out as one of two modes, and introduces a free on-ramp mode alongside it.

  • ✓ Prized Game — the mode everything else in this bible describes by default. Revised from a flat 500 Fel fee to variable stake tiers: the game creator sets an entry fee, and all players in that lobby pay the same amount. Example tiers used for expiration timing (Section 18): 100-500 Fel, 501-1,000 Fel, 1,001-2,000+ Fel. All players' fees form a pot (up to 5x the per-player fee for a full lobby). At game end, 20% of the pot is burned (permanently removed from circulation — a deliberate Fel sink) and the remaining 80% is awarded to the winner(s). Full property system (up to 10 properties, 3x3+1 blocks), the 3 Prize Cards, alliances/betrayal, and elimination all apply exactly as locked in Sections 01, 02, 09, and 14/15.
  • ✓ Higher stakes = bigger pot, naturally letting players choose their own risk/reward level — a nice emergent consequence of the tier system, not something separately designed.
  • ✓ Campaign — a free-entry mode. Same core game loop as a Prized Game (same 20x20 board shape, dice movement, PvP, alliances) but with no entry fee, no properties, and no Prize Cards — there's nothing to place at game setup and nothing hidden to find.
  • ✓ Campaign rewards: a small amount of Coins (the same personal, match-scoped currency from Prized Games) plus common-rarity cards, drawn from the same Attack/Guard/Property card pools used in Prized Games. These are collected and carried forward for use in Prized Games.
  • ✓ Campaign is primarily PvE: bots are the main opponents, not other real players. Composition is flexible — however many real players queue together, bots fill any remaining slots up to the usual player count.
  • ✓ Campaign includes NPC-exclusive locations (e.g. a Fountain) not present in Prized Games — these add challenge and serve as reward triggers, alongside the Fountain itself. Full list deferred to asset creation.
  • ✓ Purpose: Campaign is the on-ramp — new or Fel-poor players build up a small card collection and currency without risking the Prized Game entry fee. This is one of (possibly several) ways a new player affords their first Prized Game, alongside the small starting Fel grant already locked in Section 10.
  • ✓ The in-game Market sells Booster Packs, purchasable with Gems — see Section 16 for the monetization entry.
Placement note Filed under Phase 6 (Multiplayer & Lifecycle) alongside Section 19, since it's really a variant on how a game instance gets created — but it touches nearly every other system (board, properties, economy, monetization), so revisit this placement if it stops feeling right.

§ Open questions

  • Campaign board layout: Outer path ring + Town Center + Inn/Blacksmith/Church, no player-owned properties. Plus new NPC locations (e.g. a Fountain) exclusive to Campaign — specific list deferred to asset creation.
  • Campaign reward triggers: The Fountain plus other NPC locations (not the sole source) — exact list deferred to asset creation.
  • Exactly how much Coins/how many cards does a Campaign match award, and does that scale with performance (win/lose) or is it flat? (Deferred alongside other numeric balance items.)
  • Campaign alliance/winner caps: Same as Prized Games — max 3-player alliance, max 2 winners, applies even when facing bots.
  • Queueing: Fully separate — a player is in one queue (Campaign or Prized Game) at a time, not both simultaneously.
  • Property leveling impact: None — leveling is Coin-gated, not tied to entry fees, and Fel is fungible/persistent regardless of which stake tier it came from. Section 11's Fel cost table stays exactly as calibrated.
  • Custom vs. preset tiers: Any custom fee amount — the game creator sets it freely. The 100-500/501-1,000/1,001-2,000+ brackets are only used to determine expiration timing (Section 18), not selectable options.
  • Stake tier's only effect is pot size — nothing else (odds, property access, etc.) changes based on the fee chosen.
