15. Player Elimination & Persistent Assets

All 7 questions resolved
Checkbox clicks won’t be saved to disk until you connect the file.

§ What's locked

Resolved by splitting assets into two categories: permanent (real-money) ownership, which is always protected, and in-match earnings, which are won and lost through PvP.

  • ✓ Permanently-owned, real-money properties and equipment are protected — they are never transferred, seized, or put at risk through PvP, regardless of elimination.
  • ✓ The only thing that transfers on elimination is 80% of the eliminated player's accumulated in-match Coins (revised from an earlier 90/10 split) — these go to the PvP winner. The eliminated player retains the remaining 20%. This split happens at the moment of each individual elimination. Coins are a real, individually-tracked personal reward (not purely cosmetic) but are never pooled between players even in a shared win — see Section 14.
  • ✓ For cards — including a Prize Card, if the eliminated player is holding one — the PvP winner instead selects which and how many cards they want to take from the eliminated player's collection, and returns the rest. This is the mechanism by which Prize Cards actually change hands: a winner will almost always take a Prize Card if the loser is holding one, since it represents the core win reward (see Section 14).
  • ✓ Equipment and property levels are already match-scoped by design (per Section 07/21 — permanent ownership, temporary match-level power) and simply stop mattering once a player is eliminated; they are not treated as a separate transferable reward.
  • ✓ A protected property remains placed on the board after its owner is eliminated but becomes inactive for the rest of that game instance: no property card triggers for visitors, no effects for the (now absent) owner.
  • ✓ Protected properties do not become available to other players, do not get auctioned, and are not NPC-controlled — ownership is untouched, since it was never actually at stake.
  • ✓ There's no "return to inventory" step, since permanent ownership never left the player's account in the first place.
New mechanics that feed this Two things decided elsewhere now connect here: PvP battles can end in an accepted withdrawal (no winner, no elimination, no transfer) — this needs its own write-up in Section 05 (PvP Turn Structure). And an accepted withdrawal opens the door for either player to propose an alliance, optionally with a negotiated offer attached — see Section 14.
Reward system overhaul The game's core reward economy is now built around exactly 3 Prize Cards per game instance, seeded into eligible property card decks at game start (see Section 14 for the full system). Dungeons are cut entirely from scope (formerly Section 13) — this system replaces what dungeon rewards would have provided, so no dungeon-sourced cards exist to reconcile.

§ Resolved decisions

  • Remain owned but become inactive? Yes — ownership is untouched (it was never at stake), but the property is inactive on the board for the rest of that game instance.
  • Remain on the board? Yes, still placed — just inactive.
  • Become available for other players? No — permanent ownership is protected.
  • Return to the owner's inventory? N/A — it never left; permanent ownership was never in question.
  • Continue generating property effects? No — inactive for the rest of the instance.
  • Become NPC-controlled? No.
  • Get auctioned? No.
