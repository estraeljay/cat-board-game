14. Game Victory Condition

Win condition, alliances, Prize Cards, and reward splits fully resolved
Checkbox clicks won’t be saved to disk until you connect the file.

§ What's locked

The win condition is now defined: king-of-the-hill style, last player or last allied group standing.

  • ✓ Losing is defined: reaching 0 HP eliminates a player from the board-game instance.
  • ✓ Win condition: a player wins when they are the last one remaining, or when they're part of the last remaining allied group.
  • ✓ Alliances are now a formal, system-tracked mechanic (superseding an earlier "informal, untracked" decision from this session). Formed via the proximity menu introduced in Section 01: the active player selects "Propose Alliance," the target player gets an immediate accept/decline prompt, and it only takes effect if both agree.
  • ✓ Alliances can also be proposed after an accepted PvP withdrawal (see below) — either player, not just the one who was active that turn, may propose it. This lets players "test" an opponent's strength in battle before deciding whether to ally.
  • ✓ An alliance proposal can include a negotiated offer to sweeten the deal (e.g. the proposer offers something in exchange). Exact offer scope (coins only vs. cards vs. anything held) is still an open sub-question — see below.
  • ✓ An alliance is a status label only — it has zero mechanical restriction. PvP is always available against an ally, and betrayal has no extra step, cooldown, or penalty. Its purpose is purely to give the game state a definitive record of who's allied, for determining shared victory at game end.
  • ✓ A shared win occurs when two or more tracked-allied players are the last ones remaining on the board.
  • ✓ Maximum alliance size is 3 players (revised down from an earlier 5-player decision — kept tighter to help games resolve faster).
  • ✓ Maximum of 2 players can share victory — this is a hard rule, not a suggestion. If 3 allied players are the last ones standing, the game does not force a resolution (no automatic sudden-death). It's on the players themselves: they must initiate PvP against each other and betray down to 2 before a winner can be declared. A 3-player alliance mathematically cannot all win — betrayal within it is inevitable by design.
  • ✓ Reward structure: the game's primary/decisive win economy is exactly 3 Prize Cards per game instance. Coins collected throughout the game are a secondary personal reward — real, but never pooled or split; see below. Dungeons are cut entirely from scope (formerly Section 13) — this system replaces what dungeon rewards would have provided.
  • ✓ Prize Card placement: each property has a chance to contain one, but only 3 total ever get activated — seeded into 3 property decks at the start of each game instance. This gives "unique"/rarer properties real prestige: owning one that's likely to hold a Prize Card makes it a desirable place for other players to visit.
  • ✓ Holding a Prize Card at the moment the game ends means that player personally claims its full payout — this bypasses the shared-win split entirely. Even in a 2-player shared victory, Prize Cards are never divided evenly; whoever is physically holding each one keeps it.
  • ✓ Prize Cards can be stolen through ordinary PvP — no new mechanic needed. The existing "winner selects which/how many cards to take" rule (Section 15) already covers seizing a Prize Card from an eliminated opponent — including if the eliminated player is holding one, it transfers to the winner. This is the real engine behind forced betrayal: an ally who found a Prize Card has no reason to hand it over, and if they're still holding it when a shared win happens, they keep the entire prize. Turning on your own ally to take it is often the only way to get it.
  • ✓ Coins are never pooled or split, in any win scenario. Each player simply keeps whatever coins they personally collected throughout the game — a shared/allied win does not combine or divide coin totals between teammates. This is deliberate: it adds a second, independent source of greed-driven betrayal on top of the Prize Cards. An ally who's out-collected you has no obligation to share, and you have no way to force it except PvP.
  • ✓ Reward split for a shared (2-player) win, for non-Prize-Card, non-coin rewards only (e.g. any leftover non-quantifiable items): the player with the most individual PvP wins during the match picks first; the other takes what's left.
  • ✓ PvP battles can end via an accepted withdrawal: either player may propose withdrawing, the opponent can accept or deny it, and only one withdrawal attempt is allowed per battle (if denied, the battle continues to its normal conclusion). A withdrawal has no penalty for the requester, and ends the battle with no winner, no elimination, and no asset transfer. Full turn-by-turn mechanics belong in Section 05 (PvP Turn Structure).
  • ✓ On elimination (0 HP within a PvP battle), the winner takes 80% of the loser's accumulated in-match Coins (revised from an earlier 90/10 split); the loser keeps 20%. For cards — including a Prize Card, if the loser is holding one — the winner picks which/how many they want and returns the rest. This happens at the moment of each individual elimination. Permanently-owned real-money properties/equipment are always protected — see Section 15 for the full breakdown.
  • ✓ Note: this replaces the shared/pooled coin-economy framing originally assumed for Sections 09–12 (Property System/Economy/Leveling/Card Design) — coins are still a real, individually-tracked reward (not purely cosmetic), but they're personal and never pooled between players, and Prize Cards — not coins — are the decisive win condition. Those sections need revisiting when reached.
Sequencing This should be settled before the overall UI and board assets are finalized.

§ Resolved decisions

  • Being the last surviving player: Yes — this, or being part of the last remaining allied group, is the win condition.
  • Completing a quest: No — not part of the win condition.
  • Reaching a certain number of points: No.
  • Owning specific properties: No.
  • Defeating a boss: No.
  • Collecting objectives: No.
  • Accumulating coins: No.
  • Reaching the finish space: No — there's no finish line; the board loop is open-ended until one player or allied group remains.
  • Some combination of the above: No — it's a single, clean win condition, not a hybrid.

§ Open questions

  • Alliance offer scope: Coins and reward cards (property-type cards earned during play) — not the core PvP Attack/Guard deck.
  • Prize Card visibility: Visible — everyone can see who's holding a Prize Card at all times. Betrayal targets are informed, not speculative, which sharpens the tension deliberately.
